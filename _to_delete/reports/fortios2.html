1. Broad Firewall Policy

Firewall and access policy fortinet.fortios.policy.broad_accept

What was found

Enabled accept policy '1' at position 1 in 'root' allows all source addresses, destinations, and services on schedule always across all interfaces; logtraffic is disable and NAT is disable.

Why it matters

The policy permits unrestricted service access between its configured interface scopes.

In plain terms: A traffic rule (or the default action for unmatched traffic) allows much more than a business need usually requires, such as any source to any destination on any service. The firewall then no longer separates the networks on either side of it.

How it could be abused

Any source matching the interface scope can target any reachable destination and service.

Example: Malware on one laptop in a user network scans the network and reaches a database or management interface in a server network, because the broad rule lets that traffic through. The attacker never has to defeat the firewall itself.

How to fix

Constrain source and destination addresses, services, schedule, and interfaces; enable appropriate policy logging.

Before closing this finding: Confirm who owns the rule and which flows it must carry, then replace it with explicit sources, destinations and services. Until then, make sure the traffic it matches is logged and inspected (see the related areas), so misuse would at least be visible.

Technical background and references

Broad rules defeat segmentation and least privilege. They are especially dangerous when they sit early in an ordered rule base, because every later, more specific rule is then never reached. The tool reports a rule as broad only when its fields resolve completely; dynamic objects and inherited policy stay unknown.

2. Weak Administrative Access

Management access exposure fortinet.fortios.management.insecure_protocol

What was found

HTTP management is enabled on interface 'wan1' in VDOM/scope 'root' (WAN-facing); at least one enabled administrator has no effective trusted-host restriction.

Why it matters

Clear-text administrative traffic can expose credentials and device-management activity.

In plain terms: The device can be managed over an unencrypted protocol such as Telnet or plain HTTP. Everything typed or sent in such a session, including administrator passwords, crosses the network readable by anyone on the path.

How it could be abused

An attacker with reachability to the named interface can intercept or attempt access to the insecure service.

Example: An attacker who has compromised any host on the same network segment (or a switch or Wi-Fi link on the way) captures traffic while an administrator logs in, reads the password from the capture, and later logs in as that administrator.

How to fix

Remove http from this interface's allowaccess list and use HTTPS or SSH with restricted administrator trusted hosts.

Before closing this finding: Check whether management traffic is confined to an isolated management network or VPN, and whether the clear-text service is additionally restricted to specific source addresses. Those reduce, but do not remove, the exposure.

3. Insecure TLS Version

Management encryption and certificates fortinet.fortios.tls.minimum_version

What was found

Scope 'root' permits legacy TLS values: ssl-min-proto-version tlsv1-1.

Why it matters

Legacy TLS protocols have known weaknesses and do not meet modern management-plane requirements.

In plain terms: The encrypted management connection (SSH or HTTPS) still allows outdated protocol versions or algorithms that are known to be weak.

How it could be abused

An on-path attacker may target protocol downgrade or legacy cryptographic weaknesses.

Example: An attacker positioned on the network path forces or waits for a connection that uses the weak option, then attacks it offline or tampers with it. Old options also widen exposure to published protocol attacks.

How to fix

Permit only tlsv1-2 and tlsv1-3 for administrative HTTPS and related TLS services.

Before closing this finding: Confirm which clients (old monitoring or automation tools) still need legacy options, and plan to upgrade them rather than keep weak algorithms enabled.

4. Privileged administrator is not source restricted

Management access exposure fortinet.fortios.admin.trusted_hosts

What was found

Enabled super_admin account 'admin' in scope 'root' has no effective trusted-host restriction.

Why it matters

Unrestricted administrator source addresses broaden the management-plane attack surface.

In plain terms: The device's management interfaces (SSH, web GUI, API) accept connections from any address, or from an untrusted/external network, instead of only from administrator workstations or a management network.

How it could be abused

An attacker with reachability or administrative access may exploit the effective weakness.

Example: The login page of the firewall is reachable from the internet. Attackers try common and leaked passwords against it around the clock, and when a new vulnerability in the management service is published, they can exploit it before the device is patched.

How to fix

Configure restrictive trusthost and ip6-trusthost entries for the administrator.

Before closing this finding: Check for an upstream control that already limits access (management VLAN, VPN, separate out-of-band network), and confirm that strong authentication, lockout and logging are in place for the exposed service.

5. Privileged local administrator lacks MFA

Administrator authentication fortinet.fortios.admin.mfa

What was found

Local super_admin account 'admin' in scope 'root' has no enabled two-factor method.

Why it matters

A stolen password alone may be sufficient for privileged access.

In plain terms: Administrators log in with accounts stored only on this device, instead of a central identity service (RADIUS/TACACS+/SAML) with multi-factor authentication.

How it could be abused

An attacker with reachability or administrative access may exploit the effective weakness.

How to fix

Enable an approved FortiToken, email, SMS, or organization-approved federated MFA method.

Before closing this finding: Confirm how local accounts are reviewed and removed, whether MFA is enforced upstream, and that logins are logged centrally.

11. Legacy SNMP community is enabled

SNMP monitoring access fortinet.fortios.snmp.legacy_community

What was found

An enabled SNMPv1/v2c community is configured in scope 'root'; its name is redacted.

Why it matters

Community-based SNMP lacks modern per-user authentication and privacy.

In plain terms: SNMP versions 1/2c are enabled. They use a shared "community" string that works like a password but is sent in clear text; sometimes it is also a well-known default such as 'public' or 'private', or grants write access.

How it could be abused

An attacker with reachability or administrative access may exploit the effective weakness.

Example: An attacker sniffs the community string (or just guesses 'public') and reads the full device inventory, interfaces, routes and sometimes configuration. With a write community, they can change settings or download the configuration.

How to fix

Migrate managers to SNMPv3 authPriv and remove the community configuration.

Before closing this finding: Check whether SNMP is limited to monitoring hosts by ACL and whether the monitoring system supports SNMPv3 so the community can be removed.

Why it matters

In plain terms: The connection between the device and its authentication server (RADIUS, RadSec or LDAP) is not protected as intended: validation, encryption or server identity checks are disabled or weak.

Before closing this finding: Check whether the authentication traffic runs over a separately protected path, and enable certificate and server-identity validation.
